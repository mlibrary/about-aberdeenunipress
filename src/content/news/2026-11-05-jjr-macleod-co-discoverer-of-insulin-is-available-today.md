Educated in Aberdeen, the physiology professor John James Rickard (known as JJR) Macleod has saved millions of lives around the world, along with Frederick Banting, through his discovery of insulin. Their revolutionary work on insulin earned them the Nobel prize. 

After winning the prize, Macleod was accused of stealing ideas and claiming more credit that he was due, but recent research has exonerated him of these accusations.  

In this book, originally published in 1993 but refreshed by Kenneth McHardy, diabetologist Michael Williams tells Macleod's true story and restores him to his rightful place amongst the heroes of medicine.
